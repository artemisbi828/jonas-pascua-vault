

# Communication - PMO

Draft runbook for planning a communication strategy before writing an email or another business artifact.

## Purpose and status

Use this page as the parent context for selecting an approach, shaping a message, and checking that its ask and next step are clear. It is a working guide to refine over time, not a fixed script.

## Strategy workflow

1. **Name the outcome.** State what understanding, decision, or action is needed. Separate the ideal outcome from fixed requirements.
2. **Map the audience.** Note what the recipient knows, what they decide, and the constraints they face. Separate confirmed facts from your interpretations and unknowns.
3. **Choose the approach.** 

	Use Motivational Interviewing when the person is weighing a change and needs room to identify their own reasons. 
	
	Use *Never Split The Difference* when terms, constraints, or a disagreement need negotiation. Combine them only when both problems are present: understand the person's concerns first, then negotiate the actual constraints.
	
4. **Choose the artifact.** Pick the form that serves the decision: an email, meeting brief, proposal, or another business artifact. Keep the message focused on one primary outcome.
5. **Build the message.** Give the relevant context and evidence, state the impact, make the ask or proposal, and name real options or constraints. Use open questions when the answer could change the plan.
6. **Review before sending.** Check that facts are distinct from assumptions, the recipient has a meaningful choice where one exists, and the wording does not imply agreement that has not happened.
7. **Close the loop.** Record the decision, owner, and next step. Capture what worked without copying sensitive business or patient information into this guide.

## Technique guide

| method | use_when | useful_moves | guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Motivational Interviewing | The other person is ambivalent about a change or commitment. | Ask, reflect, affirm specifically, summarize, and invite their reasons. | Do not use collaboration as a cover for persuasion or a fixed decision. |
| Never Split The Difference | The conversation involves contested terms, limited resources, or a difficult constraint. | Label a possible concern, mirror key wording, ask calibrated questions, and check understanding. | A hypothesis about someone's concern is not a fact; verify it and state fixed limits honestly. |

Both approaches value listening and accurate understanding, but they are not interchangeable. MI centers the person's own motivation and agency; Voss's negotiation methods help uncover constraints and work toward terms.
