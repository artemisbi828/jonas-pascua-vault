# 2026-10-14 - Business Communication - Never Split The Difference

High-level, original notes on applying Chris Voss's negotiation ideas to plan business conversations; this is not a substitute for the book.

## Core idea

Treat a difficult conversation as information-gathering under uncertainty. Understand the other person's pressures and constraints before advocating for an outcome. A good conversation makes the real problem and the next decision clearer.

## Useful practices

- **Tactical empathy:** Show that you understand the other person's perspective without implying agreement.
- **Labels:** Tentatively name a possible concern so the other person can confirm or correct it.
- **Mirroring:** Briefly repeat a meaningful phrase to invite elaboration.
- **Calibrated questions:** Use open “what” and “how” questions to uncover constraints, criteria, and workable options.
- **Accusation audit:** Surface likely negative interpretations early and calmly, rather than letting them derail the conversation.
- **Check understanding:** Summarize the other person's view until they recognize it as accurate; do not mistake a quick “yes” for real alignment.
- **Make room for “no”:** Treat a refusal as useful information about a boundary, not as the end of inquiry.

## Planning checklist

1. Define the outcome, the decision needed, and what is non-negotiable versus flexible.
2. List the other person's likely goals and constraints; mark guesses as unknown until confirmed.
3. Acknowledge the strongest likely concern without arguing against it.
4. Prepare two or three open questions that would change the plan depending on the answers.
5. State the proposal plainly, then ask what would need to change for it to work.
6. Close with the decision, owner, and next step (including a date when useful).

## Guardrails

- Empathy is not agreement; a label is a hypothesis, not a fact.
- Use these techniques to clarify real interests, not to manufacture pressure or conceal material information.
- Conversation technique does not replace evidence, policy, authority, or clear written terms.
